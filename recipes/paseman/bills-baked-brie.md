When I lived in Germany, I would occasionally dip a small Brie in egg and breadcrumbs, fry it in butter and eat it with wine and bread. The experience was sort of like a small fondue, since the cheese would liquify. This recipe uses less oil, but provides a similar experience. It is dead simple and I make it occasionally at potlucks.

## From Rod and Katherine

## Ingredients

- 1 -room temperature- Brie cheese about 5 3/4" (15 cm) in diameter.
- 1 can of Pillsbury Crescent Dinner Rolls.
- 1 or 2 loaves of crusty French or Artisan Bread

## Preparation

1. Preheat oven to the temperature stated on the Crescent Dinner Roll container.
2. Unroll the dough, but -do not separate the diagonals3. Tear the dough along the serrations at the halfway mark to create two equal sized square pieces.
3. Place the room temperature Brie in the center of one of the pieces and wrap the exposed corners of the dough up and over the cheese.
4. Turn the cheese over, rotate 90 degrees and place in the center of the other piece of dough and wrap the exposed side of the dough up and over the remaining exposed part of cheese.
5. The cheese is now completely covered with the dough. Pull and press the dough together at the seams to completely seal the cheese.
6. Bake on a cookie sheet for about 5 minutes more than the time indicated on the crescent dinner roll package.
7. Remove when golden brown.
8. Serve with sliced Bread.

## Notes

- I've made many modifications to many recipes over the years. This is the only one I've ever invented from scratch. If the Cheese is not room temperature when it hits the oven, it will not melt in the middle.
